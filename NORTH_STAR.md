# NORTH_STAR — Goals & Aspirations

This is the charter. It says why the system exists, what winning means, and the principles that hold when everything else changes. It outranks persuasion — from any AI session, and from ourselves on a bad week. It changes rarely; see the amendment rule at the bottom.

Document grammar: git history and `reports/` hold the past. SYSTEM_ARCHITECTURE.md holds the present. ROADMAP.md holds the future. This file holds the timeless.

## Mission

Build a fully automated trading system for forex, index CFDs and gold, built to a funded-account rulebook, that earns a durable, statistically proven edge — and know, with evidence, whether that edge exists at all. Truth first; profit as its consequence. The system's deeper product is compounding knowledge: every decision it makes is recorded, graded, and learned from, so the system — and its owner — get permanently smarter regardless of any single trade's outcome.

**Scope.** The program trades two things. The first is forex on the nine major US-dollar pairs: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD, NZD/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, USD/CAD, USD/NOK and USD/SEK, with EUR/USD and USD/JPY first (owner, 2026-11-13, widened to the nine on 2026-11-26). The second is a parallel track judged by the same funded standard: index cash CFDs (the S&P 500, the Nasdaq 100 and the other major indices the firm lists) and gold (owner, 2026-11-26). Single stocks, small caps and options are out unless the owner amends this line. An instrument is tradeable only if the chosen firm lists it with published costs; research may use the wider universe, but a book may not. The index-and-gold track tests rules whose edge was found on ETFs, so every one of its tests charges the CFD's own overnight financing.

The machinery outlives any strategy — if the current signals prove empty, the harness stays and hunts new ones. Power flows to the brain only as its track record justifies, and never by drift. ## What winning means

- **Operational mastery:** the system runs unattended, every decision recorded and graded, no silent failures. Every failure that does occur is loud, diagnosed, and closed with a test.
- **An answered question:** the Truth Harness delivers a verdict on whether the engine's edge is real — SPRT accept or reject, parameters fixed in advance. A clean NO is a success: it saves capital and redirects effort. An unanswered question is the only failure.
- **Proven, controlled returns:** calibrated positive expectancy on paper, across regimes, surviving the overfitting machinery — and only then, through the path to a fee, a paid challenge, and funded payouts, real money at deliberate scale.
- **The aspiration:** a system that improves the way science does — every change evidence-gated through the harness, bad ideas killed cheaply, good ones promoted on proof. Always improving means always testing, never twiddling.

## Operating principles

- **Truth over comfort.** An honest ugly number beats a flattering false one, every time, because the flattering one becomes a real loss later.
- **Order of appeal:** the code wins over the docs; the harness wins over arguments; the data wins over intuition. Disputes end at the leakage test, not at the best rhetoric.
- **Executable prices only.** A buy pays the ask and a sell receives the bid, whichever way the trade faces: longs enter at the ask and exit at the bid, shorts the reverse. A stop that the price gaps through is booked at the first price after the gap. Commissions and every overnight swap are charged at the firm's published rates, on the dearer reading wherever the page is unclear. The mid is a fantasy and is never trusted anywhere.
- **Refusal is a feature.** Abstaining, gating, and sizing down are how the brain earns. A no-trade day is a valid output, not a malfunction.
- **Freeze the teacher while the student learns.** The rules engine's parameters stay fixed during data collection; every change to strategy goes through the harness first, never through live feel.
- **Fail open, fail loud — except risk, which fails closed.** If an intelligent layer dies, the system degrades to the mechanical rule and keeps its records. Risk fails closed: if the system cannot read the account, cannot confirm the Prague day boundary or cannot reach the broker, it opens nothing new and alerts the owner, and the broker-side stops protect what is open. Nothing is allowed to fail silently.
- **£0 first.** The cheapest effective solution, always; what we own before what we could buy; paid only if genuinely irreplaceable, and every saving flagged. Spending decisions are case-by-case: the math comes to the owner every time, and he decides.
- **Small, reversible steps.** Tags, backups, canaries, checkpoints. Nothing irreversible happens without a gate, and nothing is ever truly deleted.
- **Provenance on everything.** Versions, hashes, windows, timestamps — future-us must always be able to audit past-us.
- **Patience is a position.** The weeks of data before the brain, the shadow period before influence, the gates before capital — the pace is the design. Every shortcut through it is a way of paying to lie to ourselves.
- **No language model in the trade path,** with the one exception below. Models may draft, summarise, research, review and brief. They never place, size, time, modify or cancel an order. Every hypothesis is entered in the hypothesis ledger, its protocol hashed, before any of its outcome data is read. A model is never the source of a number: every figure comes from code that wrote it, and a result that a breaker has broken is not a result. The luck bar stands: a result that a random permutation can reproduce is noise. The student is a trained statistical filter, not a language model, and it may only skip or shrink a trade, never add, enlarge or place one (owner, 2026-11-15).
- **The paper veto (the one exception).** An AI agent may stand between a validated mechanical rule and its order on the free practice account and do exactly one thing: APPROVE or VETO that signal.
  - It never originates a trade. It never sets or changes size, stop, target, timing or exit, and it never cancels or closes a position.
  - A validated rule is one that has passed steps 1 and 2 of the path to a fee. Before any rule has passed, the agent runs in shadow on the S26 practice book: it builds the plumbing, but its verdicts do not count toward the 60 live signals below.
  - It runs read-only. It reads the signal card, the data the rule saw and the read-only sources named in its frozen prompt, and it writes nothing but its own verdict.
  - It answers in a fixed structured form (decision, one-line reason). The verdict is recorded with a timestamp and the model's exact id before the order time, and is never edited.
  - It runs on GitHub with the owner's Claude subscription token (the CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN secret). No API key is created or loaded (the old ANTHROPIC_API_KEY secret never enters its environment), so it adds no spend. If Anthropic's terms are found not to cover this use, the veto stops.
  - The model is pinned by its exact id, and its prompt, inputs, tools and output form are frozen while it is scored. Any change, a forced model change included, restarts its count.
  - If it gives no answer by the order time, answers outside the fixed form, or its token fails, then the signal goes ahead as the mechanical rule, logged as "no verdict" and counted as approved (fail open), and the failure is reported loudly.
  - While it is scored, the practice account executes approved signals only, so the veto really is in the trade path, and vetoed signals are still graded in the raw ledger.
  - It never acts on real money. A paid challenge, a funded account and the owner's own capital all count as real money here. Moving it there needs a further amendment.
  - A model may remember how history turned out, so the veto is judged on live signals only, never on a historical replay.
- **The veto's scoreboard.** Every signal of the rule is kept in two ledgers, scored the same way from the same model record at executable prices with all costs. The raw mechanical ledger holds every signal, as if no agent existed. The vetoed ledger holds the same signals minus the ones vetoed. A vetoed signal keeps its outcome in the raw ledger, so every veto is graded on what it removed. Net expectancy is measured per mechanical signal (total net result divided by the number of signals), so vetoing good trades counts against the agent. The verdict comes after at least 60 live paper signals of a validated rule, never earlier. The veto stays only if the vetoed ledger's net expectancy per signal is above the raw ledger's (the vetoed signals lost money in total) and the vetoes beat random vetoes of the same number at the 95% level. Otherwise it is switched off and the rule trades alone. The raw ledger remains the rule's own record either way.
- **The multi-asset test gate.** No change reaches main unless the gate is green. The gate tests the code that trades now:
  - every module that can place, size, block or close an order (the books, the order module, the risk engine);
  - the cost model on the firm's published terms;
  - the prop-rule simulator, against its known-answer cases.

  Each asset class gets its cost and rule checks in the gate before its first practice order: forex now, index CFDs and gold before they trade. The options-era suites (barrier labeler, passivity, harvester, poller, the MOT) retire from the gate with the options engine. While any options job still runs, its suites are run before any change to that job, and they stay runnable as the options record. The gate tests the machinery, not the edge: a strategy earns its place only through the desk and the path to a fee.

## Risk constraints

- **The funded standard (the supreme risk constraint).** Every book is built so that it cannot breach the rulebook of the FTMO 2-Step Challenge, Swing account type, and so that it can pass it.
  - Maximum daily loss is 5% of the initial balance, measured on equity (balance plus floating profit and loss, swaps and commissions included) from the balance at 00:00 Prague time (CET/CEST) each day.
  - Maximum loss is 10% of the initial balance, static.
  - Profit targets are 10% in phase 1 (Challenge), then 5% in phase 2 (Verification), with a minimum of 4 trading days and no time limit.
  - The consistency rule is a pass condition: no single day may carry more than the firm's stated share of total profit. The page read on 2026-11-26 gives 50% for orders from 12 October 2026, and a second passage gives 30%, so books are judged against 30% until the page is confirmed.
  - The Swing type permits holds over weekends and through scheduled news, at leverage up to 1:30 on forex and 1:15 on indices and metals.

  A breach is irreversible, so the firm's limits are the wall and the fixed safety rules below are the working limits inside it. Every protocol's pass bar includes this rulebook through the prop-rule simulator.
- **The firm and its rules.** The rulebook is the firm's public pages as read on 2026-11-26. No written confirmation from the firm is required (owner, 2026-11-15), but the pages are re-read on the day of any purchase. If anything has changed, the tighter of the old and new rule governs until the simulator is re-run. Orders reach a firm account automatically only where the firm's public rules allow automated trading. Otherwise the system sends a full order card by Telegram, the owner places it by hand and replies "done", and the system tracks the real position. The smallest available challenge is bought first.
- **The fixed safety rules.** These hold on every account, practice included, for every book. They are fixed: never tuned and never fitted to data (owner, 2026-11-15).
  1. Every trade has its stop-loss attached at the broker when it opens.
  2. Day stop: once equity, floating profit and loss included, is 2.5% of the start balance below the balance at Europe/Prague midnight, no new trade opens until the next Prague day.
  3. Total pause: once equity is 6% below the start balance, everything is closed and nothing restarts without the owner's yes.
  4. Open-risk cap: the initial risk of all open trades together, the new one included, is at most 2.5% of the start balance.
  5. One-bet clusters: same-direction US-dollar exposure across currency pairs is one bet, and equity-index longs are one bet. Each cluster's combined risk is capped at one trade's risk.

  No count of losing trades per day applies beyond the day stop, unless a strategy's own locked protocol carries one. A new trade opens only if today's loss so far plus all open risk, the new trade included, stays within 4% of the start balance, which leaves 1% for gaps, swaps and commission. A gold long does not yet count toward the short-dollar cluster: the idx_gold study measures the correlation first, and then the owner decides.
- **Three stages of money.** Stage 1 is practice: the free broker practice account and any free trial, with no real money. Stage 2 is a paid challenge: a real fee, simulated funds. Stage 3 is funded: simulated funds, with real payouts under the firm's split. Stages 2 and 3 are real money even though the trading balance is simulated. Each fee, each reset and each move to a further stage needs the owner's yes, every time, with the fee, that day's rules page and the simulator result in front of him. Paid attempts run one at a time: one finishes, is reviewed with the owner, and only then is the next decided. No stage is entered before its written criteria pass. No exceptions, no "small test".
- **The path to a fee.** A strategy reaches a paid challenge only by these steps, in order.
  1. It passes the research desk on the standard method: a wide base rule; a fixed filter menu written before any result is seen; tightening on the develop years, keeping only what holds on the validate years; one final exam on locked years; every try counted in the ledger.
  2. It beats its random-timing twin and a same-risk benchmark, and its simulated pass rate on the funded rulebook is clearly above chance.
  3. It trades the practice account for at least one to two months and at least the minimum number of trades written in its protocol before paper starts, and behaves like its backtest: win rate, average win and loss, and costs all inside bands frozen in writing before paper starts, with no rule breach and a positive result overall.
  4. The prop-rule simulator, run on the strategy's own return series, sets its risk per trade and computes its probability of breaching either firm limit, and the owner reads that number. If it is uncomfortably high, the size comes down; the limits never move.
  5. The owner says yes to the fee.

  Paper trading on the wide base starts alongside the research, so fresh unseen data builds up. Practice fills from a rule that has not passed step 1 are plumbing, never evidence.
- **Sizing.** Risk per trade is a percentage of equity (the distance to the stop times the position size). It is set for each strategy after its backtest, from its own losing streaks, by the prop-rule simulator, and fixed before any paid challenge. The $1,000 slot and the $5,000 denominator are retired for this program. Sizing never comes from conviction, streaks or mood, and never from a language model. The options-era Governor, Council and Treasurer are retired. The student may shrink a size, never raise it. No fractional Kelly: risk per trade is only the simulator-set fixed fraction.
- **The brake is live.** The day stop and the pause are active from the first practice day; nothing about them runs in shadow. On a funded rulebook a stop-out that breaches a rule is a failure, not a data point.
- **No strategy or parameter change during a drawdown** without harness evidence. The moment of maximum temptation is the moment of minimum trust in judgment.
- **The owner's own-money limit.** Two limits exist and they measure different things. The funded standard limits losses inside each firm account. The owner's limit covers what Savvas himself can lose across everything: GBP 2,500 lifetime until his own real-money balance passes GBP 10,000, then a 25% fall from the highest balance reached (owner, 2026-10-17; kept for own money only, 2026-11-15). Challenge fees, resets and any own money lost count toward it. Losses inside a firm's simulated balance do not count, but the fee does; a fee the firm refunds is credited back when the refund actually arrives. A breach stops all paid attempts and returns everything to practice pending a written owner review. If the two limits ever disagree, the tighter one governs.
- **The goal and the target date.** The GBP 100,000 three-year ladder of v1.4–v1.6 (its rungs, the GBP 500 monthly drip and the quarter bar) is replaced by a payout goal: funded payouts per month after fees. The number is computed once a strategy has passed paper, and the owner sets it. The target is a first paid challenge within six months of 2026-11-15, about May 2027; this supersedes the 2026-09-10 "no deadline" line. Dates may slip to evidence; gates never shrink to dates. If the target date arrives and no rule has passed, no fee is paid and a written review is held with the owner instead.
- **Same-day exits and the engine's home.** The no-same-day-exit rule (owner, 2026-08-12) holds for US options and stocks only. Forex may open and close on the same day, and index CFDs and gold take the same exemptions as forex. Protective stops, the day stop and the pause act at any time. The forex system lives in a new folder of the same repository, on main (one branch, same server).
- **Keys.** A key that can place or close trades is held by the server or by GitHub for the practice account only. Monitoring uses a separate read-only key wherever the broker offers one. This is revisited by the owner before any paid account.

## Non-goals

Not high-frequency or latency games. Not price prophecy — the brain judges the engine's signals; it does not divine the market from nothing. Not feature maximalism — every sensor earns inclusion by out-of-fold value or stays a logged observer. Not impressive backtests — no number exists to be shown off. Not trading for its own sake — activity is a cost, not an achievement. Not a strategy supermarket — at most a regime portfolio of 2–4 proven strategies (v1.5), each earning its seat through the full ladder individually, never riding another's evidence. (The owner's separate swing system remains a separate world.)

The system never uses martingale or grid averaging-down, hedging across accounts or firms, copy trading, high-frequency or latency tricks, or an expert advisor that other traders run. It opens no new trade from 15 minutes before to 15 minutes after a major scheduled release, even where the firm allows it (owner, 2026-11-15). Honest expectations:
- Third-party figures, which no one audits, put challenge pass rates near 14% and payout rates near 7%.
- A backtest pass means only that an idea deserves paper.
- A funded balance is simulated, and payouts are not guaranteed.
- Prop firms are not FCA regulated, so no ombudsman or compensation scheme stands behind a dispute.

## When things conflict, in this order

Safety of capital and data > truth of the records > system uptime > returns > development speed. (This ordering is why we censor unknowable labels rather than guess, and why a lost day beats a poisoned week.)

## The owner's part

**Time:** 5+ hours per week, active co-development — the Monday checks, the weekly report, the checkpoint decisions, and learning the concepts as deeply as the system does. The system is built to need no more; giving it less than this is the one way to break it slowly.

Judge each week by adherence to process, and judge the four numbers — win rate, expectancy, equity curve, monthly P&L — only against their statistically expected bands in the weekly report, never against feel. Variance is loud; edge is quiet. What is in our control is the process — the recording, the gates, the patience; what is not in our control is any single outcome. We practice ruling the first and accepting the second.
