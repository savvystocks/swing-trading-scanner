# PUMP — Peer pre-earnings template study (HAL, BKR) vs PUMP setup

Date: 2026-04-28
Position: PUMP $15 CALL 15 May, x8 @ $2.29 (cost basis $1,832)
Earnings: 5 May (7 cal days away). Forced exit: 4 May.

## Why this study exists

Yesterday's decision update leaned hard on "peer earnings already beat (HAL, BKR)
and PUMP is institutionally accumulated, so the directional bet has wind at its
back." That logic only holds if PUMP's pre-earnings tape looks like the peer tape
DID before they beat. So I pulled HAL's and BKR's price/volume for the 15 sessions
before each of their Q1 prints and put them next to PUMP's last 15.

The result is not what I expected and changes how I should think about this week.

## What HAL and BKR actually did into their prints

### Halliburton — beat 4/21
- 15-session run INTO earnings: **-6.6%** (yes, negative)
- Accumulation days: **0**. Distribution days: **2**.
- The day before the print HAL closed -1.3% on 137% volume — a distribution candle.
- Reaction: gapped **+3.7%**, closed day 1 **+4.0%**. Continued higher next 2 days
  to 39.65 (+8% from pre-print close).

### Baker Hughes — beat 4/23
- 15-session run INTO earnings: **+3.6%** (modest drift)
- Accumulation days: **0**. Distribution days: **1**.
- Volume was below 40-day average for 11 of 15 sessions — disinterest, not
  accumulation.
- Reaction: gapped **only +0.7%**, but closed day 1 **+3.1%**, then exploded to
  **+16%** by day 2 ($68.94 vs $59.78 pre-print) before fading back.

### Common pattern across both peers
Neither stock was being accumulated. HAL was being distributed. BKR was drifting
on light volume. **The market was NOT positioned long going in.** When the beats
landed, the move was a re-pricing — funds had to chase. That's why HAL and BKR
worked so hard post-print: there was no front-running to unwind.

## What PUMP looks like right now

- 15-session run INTO earnings: **+20.9%** (massive)
- Accumulation days: **2**. Distribution days: **0**.
- Two of the largest up-days were on 162% and 113% of avg volume — real demand.
- Net read: **INSTITUTIONAL ACCUMULATION** (the read yesterday's update used).

## The honest read: PUMP's tape is the OPPOSITE of the peer tape

| | HAL pre-print | BKR pre-print | PUMP pre-print |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15d return | -6.6% | +3.6% | +20.9% |
| Accumulation days | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| Distribution days | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| Bid already in? | NO | NO | YES |

This contradicts the read I made yesterday. The peer template is "stock that
nobody loves beats and gets re-rated higher". PUMP is "stock that everyone has
been buying for 3 weeks reports next Tuesday".

Those are DIFFERENT setups. The risk-reward looks like this:

- **HAL/BKR template** = beat causes 3-8% pop because the market is offsides.
- **PUMP template** = beat is required to hold the level it's already at. A
  beat that meets expectations could trigger profit-taking ("buy rumour, sell
  news"). Only a blowout forces another leg up.

## Counterargument: small-cap momentum names follow different rules

PUMP is $1.3B mcap, retail+momentum driven. HAL ($35B) and BKR ($55B) are
institutional mega-caps. Patterns might not transfer:

1. Small-caps can melt up into prints because there's no liquidity to absorb
   late buyers — the +21% was on relatively thin volume (2-4M shares).
2. Momentum names beat AND go higher when the narrative compounds (sector + the
   stock itself). PUMP fits this if HAL and BKR's beats validated the sector
   thesis right when PUMP was breaking out.
3. PUMP's accumulation days came AFTER HAL beat (4/21 +8.5%) and AFTER BKR beat
   (4/23 +9.2%). That's not "front-running PUMP earnings", that's "buying the
   sector validation". Different motivation, possibly different unwind risk.

So the +21% run isn't necessarily a "long crowded" signal — it could be sector
re-rating that PUMP needs to confirm with its own beat.

## Lesson candidates from this study

5. **Always pull peer pre-earnings tape before sizing a peer-tailwind thesis.**
   The "peers beat so we ride the small-cap" read sounds clean but the peer
   tape may show disinterest/distribution, which is the OPPOSITE template to
   front-run. Don't reason from the peer headline alone.
6. **A 20%+ run into earnings on a small-cap is itself a risk factor**, not just
   a momentum confirmation. Crowded longs need a blowout to extend.
7. **Sector re-rating buys are different from earnings front-running buys.**
   PUMP's accumulation days CAME AFTER peer beats, not BEFORE its own. That
   distinction matters for predicting the post-print response.
