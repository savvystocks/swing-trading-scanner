## Why we closed today instead of trimming half

Yesterday's peer-comparison journal said: trim 4 at $3.30, ride the other 4
to $4.58 or forced exit Mon 5/4. The original plan was to scale out.

Today's screenshot showed premium $3.75 with PUMP up 4.48% intraday. Pulled
the live tape: USO +4.87%, LBRT +4.13%, HAL/BKR/SLB only +0.7-1.0%, SPY
flat. The +4.48% was NOT from the earnings positioning thesis — it was from
**WTI crude breaking $100 on the Strait of Hormuz blockade crisis**. Iran/US
mutual blockade has cut shipping through the strait (~20% of global energy
flows) to near zero.

That changed the calculus from "trim to plan" to "close full":

1. **The +44% gain came from a NON-thesis driver.** The system scored PUMP
   on earnings positioning, peer beats, accumulation, RS — not oil
   geopolitics. Today's windfall was leverage to a catalyst the model didn't
   know about. Holding more for upside means betting on that windfall to
   continue, which the system has zero edge in predicting.

2. **Resolution risk over the weekend.** Trump-Iran negotiations are
   deadlocked but not dead. A breakthrough headline Saturday or Sunday
   would gap PUMP -5 to -10% Monday morning. Combined with the earnings IV
   crush priced in for Tue 5/5, the residual 4 contracts could give back
   most of the gain in two sessions.

3. **The first-trade discipline rule.** The opening journal rated this C+
   for size (22% over cap), DTE (18 below the 21d cliff), and rich IV. The
   outcome being good doesn't fix the entry. Banking the win and exiting
   restores rule-following before trade #2.

4. **Forced exit was 4 May anyway.** The remaining 4 contracts would have
   been closed on 4 May regardless. We saved 5 trading days of theta drag,
   2 days of weekend gap risk, and the binary print outcome on 5 May.

The fill was at $3.30 mid not $3.75 because the bid was around $3.20-3.30
(spread had widened intraday with the move). Net difference vs holding to
$3.75 mid: ~$360 left on the table per 8 contracts, or about $45/contract.
Acceptable cost of getting the trade off the books cleanly.

## What worked

1. **The pre-committed exit ladder.** $3.30 was the trim trigger from
   yesterday's peer journal. When the trigger fired, we acted — not deferred,
   not renegotiated, not "let me see if it goes to $4 first". This is the
   discipline that turns a winning system into a winning account.

2. **The peer-template study (4/28) saved us from over-confidence.** That
   study correctly flagged that PUMP entered earnings overcrowded vs.
   HAL/BKR's underowned setups. Win prob revised to 37%. The trim level
   moved from +100% to +44%. THAT IS WHY we got out today instead of
   waiting for a $4.58 target that was statistically unlikely to land
   pre-earnings.

3. **Reading today's tape, not just the price.** The USO/LBRT/peers print
   showed this was a sector beta move, not a PUMP-specific catalyst. That's
   what flipped the decision from "trim half" to "close all".

4. **Deep ITM strike captured the move.** The $15 strike with delta 0.80
   participated 1:1 in the underlying move. A $17.50 strike with delta 0.50
   would have produced about half the dollar profit on the same underlying
   move — even though it would have shown a bigger percentage.

## What didn't

1. **Original entry was still C+.** 18 DTE inside the 21d cliff, $1,832
   over the $1,500 cap, IV at 90% on entry. Outcome doesn't validate the
   entry — the entry got rescued by a Hormuz crisis nobody saw coming on
   4/27. Future trades must respect the picker rules regardless.

2. **No hedge for the binary risks.** We had earnings 5 May and Hormuz
   resolution risk both unhedged. A $20 short call against the position
   would have capped upside but offset some of the IV crush risk. For trade
   #2, consider whether collared structures are worth it on event-driven
   plays.

3. **The system has no geopolitical signal.** The Hormuz news has been
   building for 7 sessions per the WTI press. None of the 7 pillars or 4
   gates flagged this. We caught the move purely because PUMP scored on
   earnings positioning that happened to be high-beta to the same
   commodity. Lucky correlation, not skill.

## Lessons for trade #2

1. **The 21-day DTE cliff is a hard rule.** This trade entered at 18 DTE
   and worked because of speed of the move. Most trades won't have a
   geopolitical accelerant — at 18 DTE with no catalyst, theta would have
   eaten the gain. Keep entries 28-42 DTE.

2. **Position cap discipline.** $1,500 not $1,832. The 22% overrun
   amplified both the gain (+$808 vs +$662 if sized to cap) and the
   would-be loss had it gone wrong (-$917 vs -$750 max-loss tolerance).
   On a long-run sample size, cap discipline matters more than upside
   capture on any single trade.

3. **Pre-commit and execute.** The exit price of $3.30 was set in the
   peer journal 24h before it fired. When it fired, we didn't debate — we
   pulled the trigger. Future trades: write the exit ladder in the
   opening journal, then EXECUTE without renegotiating.

4. **Peer-tape is essential context.** The peer comparison study (4/28)
   was the single most valuable journal of this trade. It dropped the win
   prob from 50% to 37% and the trim trigger from +100% to +44%. Without
   it, we'd have held for +100% and likely watched the gain evaporate
   into earnings + Hormuz reversal. Make peer-tape standard for any
   pre-earnings position going forward.

5. **System-level signal: oil/commodity exposure.** PUMP's beta to oil
   was unmodelled. For energy small-caps, a "WTI delta" signal would be
   useful — pull the rolling correlation between the ticker and CL=F /
   USO over 60 days, score it. When oil moves >2% on a session, energy
   small-caps with high WTI delta should ALL be flagged in the dashboard.

6. **News-driven moves are a separate exit signal.** The +4% today wasn't
   from the earnings setup — it was from oil. When the catalyst that's
   driving the move ISN'T the catalyst the system identified, that's a
   signal to take profit faster. The thesis didn't earn the move; the
   tape did. Take what the tape gives you.
