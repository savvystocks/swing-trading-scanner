## PART 1 - THE INCOME BOOK: the credit spread at $1,600

### What the trade is

Once a week, on the index (XSP, cash-settled, no assignment):

- SELL a put about 2% below the market
- BUY a put about 4% below the market
- Hold both to Friday's expiry; they settle themselves

The live example on the books right now: sold the 745 put at $1.61, bought the 729 put at $0.56.
The strikes are 16 points apart, so **the $1,600 allocation IS one contract** - the width defines
it. Credit collected $105. Collateral held $1,495. Maximum loss $1,495.

### What happens at expiry

| Where the index settles | What that means | Result |
|---|---|---|
| above 745 | fell less than 2.6% | **keep $105** |
| 740 | fell 3.3% | -$395 |
| 737 | fell 3.7% | -$695 |
| 729 or below | fell 4.7% or more | **-$1,495**, the maximum |

### The probabilities

| | Source |
|---|---|
| **88% of weeks win** | 2.5-year backtest, 114 weeks |
| 5 of 5 live weeks won | live record since 2026-08-19 |
| Average winning week | +$105 |
| Average losing week | -$585 (implied by the backtest identity) |
| Worst week in 114 | -$929 - the full -$1,495 never occurred |
| Expected value | **+$20.19 a week per contract**, ~$87 a month |

### The regime rule that does the heavy lifting

Measured 2026-08-25 on real index quotes and weekly settles:

| Market regime | Result per week |
|---|---|
| MILD | **+$64, t +4.5, 94% win** |
| BULL | +$9 - noise |
| BEAR | **-$140, worst week -$923** |

The engine stands the trade down in BEAR. That single rule is why the strategy works, and it is
also its weakest point: the gate reads the index against its 50-day average, so it disengages
*after* a fall begins, not before.

## PART 4 - THE RISKS, NAMED

1. **The income book is one bet held N times.** Every credit spread expires the same Friday on the
   same index at the same strikes. Four contracts is not diversification, it is leverage. **The fix
   is an expiry ladder** - hold positions expiring on four different Fridays so a single bad week
   realises one of them and the other three have time to recover. This is designed but not built
   and it is the highest-priority build in the plan.
2. **The regime gate lags.** It reads the index against its 50-day average, so a fast fall from a
   calm market hits before the gate disengages. The backtest contains no crash.
3. **Consecutive bad weeks, not one.** Four losing weeks in a row is the scenario that erases two
   years of income-book gains. The 114-week sample never contained one.
4. **The directional book is 65% losers by construction.** It makes money on a fat tail, which
   means long flat stretches and the constant temptation to interfere.
5. **The basis risk.** Everything from the spread studies is close-to-close and measured 18.6 points
   optimistic against the court's basis on this filter. The plan above uses **only v3 numbers** for
   the directional book, and treats the +5.5-point spread gain as upside that is not in the table.
6. **Nothing here is proven live.** The credit spread has five weeks. The directional seats as
   spreads have zero. The court has promoted nothing.

## PART 5 - THE FAIL-SAFES

### Already built and running

| | What it does |
|---|---|
| Hard stop at -50% | every directional position, checked every 10 minutes; no trade can go to zero |
| Broker-side stops | a resting stop order behind every position, so a dead engine still exits |
| Trail at +50%, 20% giveback | banks winners instead of round-tripping them |
| Expiry close at 3 days | no position rides into settlement |
| Regime gate | stands the credit spread down in BEAR |
| No same-day sells | owner rule, enforced everywhere |
| One record per contract | the occ-collision guard |
| One position per underlying | per strategy, plus a same-day block across all books |
| Halt and flatten | Telegram commands, authenticated |
| Daily brake | trips after 3 stop-outs or a 2x allocation loss; currently in shadow, measuring |
| Watchdog + page bundles | stall detection, auto-rollback to the last good code |

### Owed before real money

1. **The expiry ladder** for the income book - the single most valuable unbuilt thing.
2. **A per-underlying aggregate cap** across all books. Both per-underlying guards were scoped to
   one strategy on 2026-09-15, so nothing currently caps total exposure to one name.
3. **The cancelled-run alarm** - a run cancelled at its timeout is not a failure to GitHub, so it
   is not a failure to any alarm that reads the sentinel.
4. **The ratchet's cancel confirmation** - the latent twin of the bug fixed on 2026-09-15.
5. **The limit cap on synthesized legs** - fills printed 31% below their limits; harmless on paper,
   not on real money.

## PART 7 - WHAT THIS PLAN IS NOT

It does not reach £100,000 in three years. At 65% deployment it reaches about $44,000 from $19,400
of deposits. The charter's path to £100,000 was never compounding this account - it was proving a
method at $5,000 and then scaling capital against a proven method. This plan is the proving stage,
and its job is to produce a method worth scaling, not to produce £100,000.

The honest summary of the whole week: we now know precisely where the edge is, precisely why the
$1,000 slot cannot reach it, and precisely which two structural changes recover most of it. What we
do not yet have is a single live trade of the thing the data actually recommends.
