### PILLAR 1 — TREND FOLLOWING

Confirm institutional momentum alignment. We trade with the dominant flow, never against it.

- Weekly and Daily timeframe alignment required: 20 EMA > 50 EMA > 200 SMA on the daily.
- On the weekly: price must be above the 30-week MA (equivalent to the 150-day SMA), and the 30-week MA must be rising.
- The 20 EMA must slope upward for at least 10 consecutive sessions.
- Regime classification: Trending, Mean-Reverting, or Choppy. Only enter in Trending regimes.
- Apply **Minervini's Trend Template** as the primary filter (see Section 4 for full detail). A stock failing the Trend Template does not pass Pillar 1.

**FAIL:** Price below 200 SMA. Death cross (50 MA below 200 MA). 20 EMA declining. Choppy or mean-reverting regime.

### PILLAR 2 — VOLATILITY BREAKOUT (VOL ORB)

Identify multi-week compression preceding an imminent directional expansion.

- Bollinger Band Width at or near a 3-month low on the weekly chart — the squeeze.
- 14-day ATR below its own 20-day average — compression confirmed.
- TTM Squeeze: Keltner Channel nested inside Bollinger Bands on the weekly.
- The squeeze fires when Bollinger Bands expand beyond Keltner Channels. Trade in the direction of expansion.
- A weekly-timeframe squeeze produces dramatically larger and more sustained moves than a daily squeeze alone.
- **Base quality matters:** The tighter and quieter the consolidation within the squeeze (weekly closes within 1–2% of each other), the more explosive the breakout tends to be. Institutional accumulation produces tight, quiet bases.

**FAIL:** Bands already wide and expanded — the move has happened. Never chase post-breakout extended stocks on this pillar alone.

**FAIL:** Premium to both own history and peer group with no quantifiable catalyst. Earnings estimates revised downward in the last 30 days with no recovery catalyst.

### PILLAR 4 — STATISTICAL ARBITRAGE (STATARB)

Confirm relative divergence and outperformance vs co-integrated peers.

- Identify 3–5 sector peers. Calculate 20-day rolling Z-score of the candidate vs the peer basket.
- For longs: Z-score below -1.5 (undervaluation relative to peers with convergence expected).
- **RS Score** = Candidate 20-day return minus SPY 20-day return. Require RS > +3.0% for entry. The stock must already be demonstrating it wants to move. This system does not buy weakness hoping for reversal — it buys strength showing it is leading.
- The divergence must have a stock-specific explanation (overreaction to temporary bad news, sector rotation, earnings miss that guided above). A Z-score gap from a genuine fundamental deterioration is not a mean-reversion trade.

**FAIL:** RS Score below −5% with no reversal catalyst. Divergence caused by confirmed fundamental deterioration.

### 4.1 — Minervini's Trend Template

Mark Minervini is one of the most successful competitive traders in history, producing 220% average annual returns across 5 years. His Trend Template is the foundational screen that selects only stocks in confirmed Stage 2 advances. A stock must satisfy ALL of the following to pass:

1. Current price above both the 150-day (30-week) and 200-day (40-week) moving averages.
2. 150-day MA above the 200-day MA.
3. 200-day MA trending upward for at least 1 month (the long-term trend is accelerating, not decelerating).
4. 50-day MA above both the 150-day and 200-day MA.
5. Current price at least 30% above the 52-week low.
6. Current price within 25% of the 52-week high.
7. Relative Strength Rating ≥ 70 (the stock has outperformed at least 70% of all stocks over the trailing 12 months).

A stock passing the Trend Template is in a confirmed Stage 2 advance with institutional sponsorship. These are the stocks that produce 50–300% moves within 6–18 months. Buying stocks outside the Trend Template is statistically much less likely to reach the 50% target.

### 4.2 — Weinstein Stage Analysis

Stan Weinstein's stage framework identifies which part of the market cycle a stock is in. Only one stage is suitable for entry.

- **Stage 1 — Basing:** Stock has stopped falling and is moving sideways. 30-week MA is flat. Volume is low. Do not enter — institutional accumulation may or may not be occurring.
- **Stage 2 — Advancing:** Stock breaks above the 30-week MA on above-average volume. The 30-week MA begins rising. This is the only stage to buy. All of the system's targets are achieved in Stage 2.
- **Stage 3 — Topping:** Stock starts moving sideways at high price. 30-week MA flattens or begins to curl down. Volume is erratic. Begin exit planning. Do not enter new long positions.
- **Stage 4 — Declining:** Stock breaks below the 30-week MA. Do not buy, never average down. Exit any remaining positions immediately.

The transition from Stage 1 to Stage 2 — when a base breaks out on surging volume with the 30-week MA beginning to turn upward — is the single most powerful swing trade entry in the entire system. This is the exact moment institutional money is committing to a new position.

### 4.3 — Base Pattern & Base Counting

The base is the consolidation period before a major move. Understanding base quality and stage is essential.

**Base Patterns (in order of reliability for 50%+ moves):**
- **Cup with Handle:** A rounded bottom forming over 7–65 weeks, followed by a 1–2 week "handle" that drifts down on declining volume. The breakout above the handle pivot is the entry. The longer and rounder the cup, the more significant the base.
- **Flat Base:** Tight horizontal consolidation of 5–6 weeks after a prior advance. Very tight weekly closes (within 10–15% range). Breakout above the upper bound of the flat base.
- **Ascending Base:** Three pullbacks to progressively higher lows, forming higher lows and higher highs simultaneously. Breakout above the third pivot high.
- **Double Bottom:** Two lows at approximately the same price, separated by a rally. The second low should undercut the first slightly (shakes out weak holders). Entry on the breakout above the middle peak between the two lows.

**Base Counting (O'Neil principle):**
- 1st stage base (after a significant prior decline): highest probability of a major move. Institutions are establishing new positions. These produce the biggest percentage gains.
- 2nd stage base: still high probability. The stock has proven itself once and institutions add to existing positions.
- 3rd+ stage base: failure rate increases dramatically. The stock is maturing, institutional ownership is high, the easy money has been made. Treat 3rd stage bases as Tier 3 at best.

**Quality indicators within a base:**
- Weekly closes tightly clustered (within 1–2% of each other for 3+ weeks) = institutional support and minimal selling pressure.
- Volume dries up to multi-month lows during the base = sellers exhausted.
- Pocket Pivot occurring within the base (see Section 4.7) = institutional accumulation signal before the breakout.

### 4.4 — Fibonacci Retracement Entry Zones

Buying at base breakouts (high price) is the riskiest entry method. Fibonacci retracement entries dramatically improve the reward/risk ratio by entering at defined pullback levels within an established uptrend.

**The three entry zones:**
- **38.2% retracement:** Shallowest pullback. Enter when price reaches the 38.2% retracement of the prior impulse wave and volume confirms support. Stop just below the 50% level.
- **50% retracement:** The "golden mean" retracement. Most common institutional re-entry point after a strong impulse wave. Strong entry with tight stop below the 61.8% level.
- **61.8% retracement (Golden Ratio):** Deepest acceptable pullback in a healthy uptrend. Enter at 61.8% with stop below the prior swing low. If price falls through 61.8% on high volume, the trend is likely broken.

**Using Fibonacci for target projection:**
- Measure the prior impulse wave (from swing low to swing high).
- Project the same distance upward from the retracement low: this gives the 100% extension target.
- The 161.8% extension is Phase 2 runner territory.
- Fibonacci extensions are not magic — they work because institutional algorithms and large traders use them, making them self-fulfilling at significant levels.

**Application:** Only use Fibonacci entries within a confirmed Stage 2 advance. Never use Fibonacci to try to catch a falling stock — always ensure Pillar 1 (Trend Template) passes before applying Fibonacci entries.

### 4.5 — Multi-Timeframe Entry Precision (MTF)

The three-timeframe cascade eliminates the majority of false entries by requiring alignment across different time horizons.

**Weekly timeframe — Direction:**
Is the stock in a Stage 2 advance with a rising 30-week MA? Is a base forming on the weekly? The weekly chart determines whether the trade should even be considered.

**Daily timeframe — Setup:**
Has the stock pulled back to a defined Fibonacci level or key support (20 EMA, 50 EMA, VPOC, anchored VWAP)? Is the base forming on the daily? Is there a recognisable pattern (bull flag, tight consolidation, inside day)? The daily confirms the setup exists.

**4-Hour timeframe — Trigger:**
Enter only when the 4-hour chart provides a momentum confirmation signal in the direction of the trade. Valid triggers:
- MACD bullish crossover on the 4-hour.
- RSI crossing above 50 from below on the 4-hour.
- A 4-hour candle that closes above a defined resistance level with above-average 4-hour volume.

This cascade from weekly to daily to 4-hour reduces premature entries — the stock looks right on the daily but the 4-hour shows it is still in intraday downtrend. Waiting for 4-hour alignment before entering cuts the stop-out rate significantly.

### 4.6 — Post-Earnings Announcement Drift (PEAD)

One of the most documented and persistent market anomalies in academic finance (Ball & Brown 1968, Bernard & Thomas 1989, continuously replicated). Stocks that beat EPS estimates continue to drift in the direction of the beat for 30–60 days. The market systematically under-reacts to earnings surprises, creating a repeatable edge.

**The PEAD trade setup:**
1. Company reports earnings that beat consensus EPS by 10%+ AND raises forward guidance.
2. Wait 1–3 trading days after the earnings announcement for the initial volatility to settle. Never enter on the gap-open day — the price action is too erratic.
3. Ensure the post-earnings stock is not already up 40%+ from pre-earnings levels — if it is, the market has already priced in the beat and there is little drift remaining.
4. Enter when the stock consolidates for 1–3 days post-earnings and begins to resume its upward move on normal or above-average volume.
5. The PEAD setup combines naturally with Pillar 6 (ERM) — a beat-and-raise is the fundamental trigger for both.

**Why PEAD produces 50%+ moves:** When a small or mid-cap company beats and raises significantly, institutional investors who were underweight must build positions over days and weeks, not minutes. This mechanical buying creates the drift. The larger the earnings surprise, the longer and stronger the drift.

### 4.7 — Pocket Pivot Buy Signal

Developed by Chris Kacher and Gil Morales. The pocket pivot identifies unusual institutional buying that occurs before a breakout — giving an early entry signal with a tighter stop than a traditional breakout entry.

**Definition:** A pocket pivot occurs on an up day where the volume exceeds the highest volume of any down day in the prior 10 trading sessions.

**Interpretation:** On a normal up day, volume is unremarkable. On a pocket pivot day, a surge of buyers absorb all available selling pressure at that price level. This indicates an institution is aggressively building a position before the breakout becomes obvious to the market.

**Entry rules:**
- The pocket pivot must occur within a base or at a support level (above the 10-week MA).
- Do not trade pocket pivots occurring in extended stocks already up 20%+ from their base breakout.
- The stop is just below the 10-day or 21-day EMA, making this a very tight-risk entry.
- A stock showing multiple pocket pivots within the same base is under sustained institutional accumulation — extremely high conviction.

### 4.8 — Insider Cluster Buying (Form 4)

Multiple insiders buying in the same stock within a 30-day window is one of the strongest predictive signals in equity markets. Insiders know their business better than any analyst. When three or more insiders buy (CEO, CFO, directors) at around the same time, they are collectively signalling they believe the stock is undervalued at the current price.

**Form 4 screening rules:**
- Look for 3+ insiders purchasing shares in the open market within a 30-day window.
- CEO or CFO purchases carry maximum weight. They have the clearest view of earnings trajectory.
- Only count open-market purchases. Automatic plan purchases (10b5-1) and option exercises with same-day sale are not meaningful signals.
- Ignore insider selling — it happens for many personal reasons (tax, diversification, house purchase). Selling is not a short signal.
- The most powerful setup: insider cluster buying occurring at a key technical support level (near the base low, at the 200-day MA) shortly before an expected catalyst.

Form 4 filings are publicly available within 2 business days of the transaction at SEC.gov. This data is free, current, and consistently exploitable.

### 4.9 — Price/Volume Accumulation Analysis

The quality of a stock's price/volume behaviour reveals institutional intent before the breakout becomes visible in price alone.

**Accumulation pattern (bullish):**
- Up days consistently accompanied by above-average volume.
- Down days on below-average or declining volume (sellers are absent or weak).
- The stock "closes in the upper half of its daily range" on the majority of sessions.
- This pattern means institutions are buying every dip and holding through any selling.

**Distribution pattern (bearish — exit signal):**
- Up days on declining volume (no conviction behind the rally).
- Down days on surging volume (heavy selling into strength).
- The stock gaps up but closes near the daily low — institutions are selling the gap.

**Up/Down Volume Ratio:**
Calculate total volume on up days divided by total volume on down days over the trailing 20 sessions. A ratio above 1.5 is accumulation. Below 0.8 is distribution. This is a quantifiable institutional sentiment signal.

**The "quiet week" signal:** When a stock trades in its tightest weekly range (top to bottom) in months on the lowest weekly volume in months — right before a breakout — this is "calm before the storm." Institutions have absorbed all available supply. Any catalyst will result in an explosive, one-directional move because there are no sellers left at this price level.

## SECTION 6 — EV & KELLY CRITERION

**EV = (Win Probability × Average Win %) − (Loss Probability × Average Loss %)**

With a 50% target and 12% stop:

| Setup | Win Rate | Avg Win | Avg Loss | EV per trade |
|-------|----------|---------|---------|--------------|
| Tier 3 (4 pillars) | ~48% | 50% | 12% | +17.8% |
| Tier 4 (5 pillars) | ~55% | 65% | 12% | +30.5% |
| Tier 5 (6–7 pillars + Tier A catalyst) | ~62% | 80% | 12% | +45.1% |

These are conservative estimates. The runner protocol (Phase 2) adds additional upside beyond Phase 1, increasing average win materially on strong setups.

Reject any setup with negative EV. Hard filter.

**Kelly % = (Win Prob × Payoff Ratio − Loss Prob) / Payoff Ratio**

Use half-Kelly in practice. Kelly assumes perfect probability estimation — in reality, estimates have error. Half-Kelly preserves the growth optimality of Kelly while protecting against estimation error.

At **+50% gain**, sell **40% of the position**. This locks in significant profit and de-risks the runner psychologically and mathematically.

- Any position reaches 12% loss from entry: hard stop triggered. No exceptions. No averaging down. No waiting for "one more day."
- Monthly EV recalculation turns negative: close or materially reduce position.

If any answer is no, the position is a candidate for immediate closure or reduction. Do not rationalise. If you would not enter it fresh, you should not be holding it.

## SECTION 11 — LIMITATIONS

A 50% per-trade target produces a natural win rate of 45–62% depending on tier and market regime. The system's edge is the 4:1+ reward/risk ratio compounded across many trades. Losing trades are expected and budgeted for. Three consecutive losses are not system failure — they are within the normal distribution of expected outcomes. Abandon the system only if the methodology, not the results of individual trades, is provably flawed.

RVOL and options flow require near-real-time data. Short interest is published twice monthly — a snapshot, not a live signal. Form 4 filings are timely (2 business days) but check for 10b5-1 plan flags.

For UK traders: 0.5% stamp duty on UK purchases, FX spread on GBP/USD conversions, CGT on realised gains. On a 50% target these costs are proportionally small but non-zero.

Use half-Kelly. Probability estimates are estimates. Markets evolve. Constant iteration is required.
